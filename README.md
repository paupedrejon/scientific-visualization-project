# How green is Europe's electricity?

Scientific Visualization project (EU-27, 2010–2025).  
We look at how the electricity mix has changed, how countries differ, and how renewable / low-carbon shares relate to lifecycle carbon intensity.

**Students:** Pau Pedrejon · Clara Maltex · Mohammed Mahmoud

## What we studied

1. How has the EU-27 electricity mix evolved since 2010?
2. Which countries have higher shares of renewables, nuclear or fossil generation?
3. Does renewable share correlate with carbon intensity? What changes if we include nuclear (low-carbon share)?
4. Can country electricity portfolios be grouped in a simple exploratory way (K-means + PCA)?

## Dataset

- **Source:** Our World in Data, Energy dataset, version **2026-09-10**
- **CSV:** https://catalog.ourworldindata.org/garden/energy/2026-09-10/owid_energy/owid_energy.csv  
  (also saved locally as `data/owid_energy.csv`)
- **Electricity indicators:** mainly from Ember (via OWID)
- **Scope:** EU-27 countries (ISO3 codes), years 2010–2025  
  → **432** country–year rows (27 × 16)

Important definitions:

- Shares are of **electricity generation**, not of total energy use.
- **Low-carbon** = renewables + nuclear.
- Carbon intensity is **lifecycle gCO₂eq per kWh** generated.

We checked that the variables we use are complete for all 27 countries in 2025 (`outputs/missingness_2025_eu27_audit.csv`). We did not fill in missing values.

## How we analyzed it

- Filter EU-27 and years 2010–2025.
- EU trends: country shares **weighted by electricity generation (TWh)**.
- For 2025: Spearman correlations between  
  - renewables vs intensity  
  - low-carbon vs intensity  
- Exploratory clustering: standardize solar, wind, hydro, nuclear and fossil shares → K-means (choose *k* with silhouette for *k* = 2…5) → PCA for a 2D plot.

Main script: `analysis.py`  
Summary of numbers we use in the talk: `outputs/verified_findings.md`  
Optional detail on the 2025 correlations and clustering: `outputs/AUDIT_2025.md`

## Main results (2025 cross-section / 2010–2025 trends)

- From 2010 to 2025 the long-term transition is clear: renewables rose a lot and fossil fell a lot (generation-weighted EU shares). In **2025 alone**, renewable share was slightly lower than in 2024 and fossil share was slightly higher — more a pause than a reversal.
- Lifecycle intensity is much lower in 2025 than in 2010 (about 210 vs 369 gCO₂eq/kWh, generation-weighted).
- Renewables vs intensity: Spearman **ρ ≈ −0.46** (n = 27).  
  Low-carbon vs intensity: **ρ ≈ −0.93** (n = 27).  
  So including nuclear strengthens the association. This is descriptive, not causal.
- France is a useful example: relatively low renewables, but very low intensity; with nuclear included it sits among high low-carbon countries (see figures 04 and 05).
- Clustering: best silhouette among *k* = 2…5 is **k = 5**, but silhouette ≈ **0.30** (weak). We treat clusters as exploratory only.

Charts are in `outputs/figures/` (generated by `analysis.py`).

## How to run

```bash
pip install -r requirements.txt
python analysis.py --year 2025
```

This downloads the CSV on first run if needed, then writes tables and PNGs under `outputs/`.

## Limitations

- Correlations do not prove causality.
- Country points are unweighted in the 2025 scatter/cluster plots (each country = one observation).
- Clustering separation is weak; do not over-interpret cluster labels.
- The five clustering features are not the full mix (e.g. bioenergy is not included separately).
- Generation shares are not the same as electricity consumption (imports matter).
